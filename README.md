## Maxim Yudin

Database administrator and data engineer. Saint Petersburg, Russia.

Thirty years in data infrastructure: banking, federal retail, lottery and gaming
platforms, systems integration.

Now running [WARP.D](https://warpd.ru) - a practice doing data engineering
and managed DBA work.

### What I work with

- **Databases** - MS SQL Server, PostgreSQL, ClickHouse
- **Lakehouse** - Apache Iceberg, Trino, S3-compatible storage
- **Pipelines** - Apache Kafka, SeaTunnel, SSIS
- **Observability** - Grafana, Prometheus, VictoriaMetrics
- **Languages** - T-SQL, Go

### What I care about

The question I ask about any new idea is not how well it demos, but what breaks
in production and who answers for it at three in the morning. Most of what I write
comes out of incidents rather than release notes.

### Repositories

- [iceberg-lab](https://github.com/maxpiter/iceberg-lab) -
  a one-command Docker stand for learning Apache Iceberg together with access control:
  Trino as the single entry point, Lakekeeper catalog, OpenFGA permissions checked
  through OPA, Keycloak SSO, RustFS as S3. Step-by-step chapters on DBeaver login,
  granting access, snapshots and time travel, table maintenance (docs in Russian)

- [ssrs-pbirs-grafana-dashboard](https://github.com/maxpiter/ssrs-pbirs-grafana-dashboard) -
  Grafana dashboards for SQL Server Reporting Services and Power BI Report Server:
  failed subscriptions with error traces, slow reports, background `.pbix` activity

### Writing

Technical write-ups, incident post-mortems and architecture notes:
[warpd.ru/blog](https://warpd.ru/blog/) | [Habr](https://habr.com/ru/users/Maxpiter/)

Topics I keep returning to: what actually breaks in AlwaysOn availability groups,
why data warehouses fail on OLTP engines, where local LLMs earn their keep and where
they burn money, and how Iceberg catalogs quietly decide who owns your data.
